01 / PROFILE & EXPERTISE

Grid physics meets data-driven engineering.

I am an electrical engineering Ph.D. researcher at Dartmouth College, following doctoral research at the University of Connecticut. My work focuses on modern distribution grids with high penetrations of distributed energy resources.

I develop methods that combine physical grid models with robust estimation, mathematical optimization, and machine learning. My research spans distribution-system visibility, grid-model correction, adaptive voltage control, autonomous DER dispatch, and cyber-resilient operation.

I have worked across university, national-laboratory, and industry research environments, including collaborations with ABB U.S. Corporate Research Center, the National Laboratory of the Rockies, Eaton, and Eversource. I value methods that are mathematically grounded, computationally practical, and validated on realistic utility systems.

02 / SELECTED RESEARCH

From grid measurements to practical control.

01

Bus-Admittance Matrix Estimation

Developed a physics-constrained GAT and uncertainty-aware WLS/MAP estimator for utility grid-model correction. The approach reduced 666 candidate bus pairs to 94-95 and improved original utility parameters by 54.1%/61.3%; the related patent application is under review.

ABB USCRC · 2026
02

Online Model-Free DER Dispatch

Created a measurement-feedback dispatch method using recursive voltage-sensitivity estimation and chance-constrained optimization. With more than 50% PV penetration, each control ran in 0.012 seconds and limited curtailment to 1.82%, compared with 4.07% under traditional Volt-Var control.

UCONN · 2022-25
03

Local Autonomous Volt-Var Optimization

Designed a communication-free, machine-learning DER dispatch scheme for 75% PV penetration. SVM and ANN models reached 98% and 96% accuracy and were validated on a real 3,384-node Colorado distribution system.

NLR + EATON · 2024
04

Cyber-Resilient Energy Delivery

Built grid-aware mitigation methods for DER cyberattacks in an eight-institute collaboration. A topology-aware sensitivity adjustment reduced power curtailment under topology changes from 15% to below 3% and reduced overvoltage nodes to 1% in a real system.

DARTMOUTH · 2024-NOW
